# Source Evaluation & Classification Protocol

All sources cited in research projects must be evaluated and classified according to the tiering system below.

## 🥇 Source Hierarchy

### Tier 1 — Primary & Authoritative
- **Peer-Reviewed Journals**: IEEE, Nature, Science, ACM, quarterly academic reviews.
- **Primary Data / Raw Measurements**: Public filings (SEC 10-K), official sensor logs, benchmark datasets, repository source code.
- **Official Regulatory / Statutory Documentation**: Legislation, official central bank reports, court filings.

### Tier 2 — High-Credibility Secondary Analysis
- **Institutional Industry Reports**: Specialized technical research firms (Gartner, McKinsey, specialized research labs).
- **Reputable Journalism / Deep Investigative Reporting**: Financial Times, Reuters, Bloomberg, verified long-form investigative outlets.
- **Domain Expert Technical Writings**: Deep technical whitepapers written by proven industry domain experts.

### Tier 3 — Informational & Contextual
- **Corporate Blogs / Press Releases**: Company marketing material, product launch posts (must be treated with skepticism regarding claims).
- **Industry Trade Publications**: High-level news commentary, general technology magazines.

### Tier 4 — Low-Credibility / Pure Noise (DISQUALIFIED)
- **Social Media Posts / Tweets / Reddit Threads**: Anonymous opinions, unverified rumors.
- **SEO Content Farms / Marketing Blogs**: Clickbait articles, generic affiliate content.

> **Rule**: Tier 4 sources must NEVER be used to support core assertions or data points in research notes.
